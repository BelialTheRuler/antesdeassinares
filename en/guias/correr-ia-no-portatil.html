The rule: VRAM decides

It isn't the processor or system RAM. What decides whether a model runs is graphics card memory, VRAM. If the model doesn't fit, it either won't start or it crawls.

The trick that makes this possible is quantisation: storing the model's weights at lower precision, typically 4 bits instead of 16. Little quality is lost and the size drops to roughly a quarter.

What fits where

  • 8 GB VRAM — small models and voice. Kokoro runs without a dedicated GPU at all.
  • 12 GB — Ministral 3 8B in 4-bit. The realistic entry point for useful text.
  • 16 GB — gpt-oss-20b. This is where most people want to land.
  • 24 GB or more — Qwen3 VL 32B and friends. Expensive dedicated card territory.
  • Above that — the "Max" versions with hundreds of billions of parameters need 400 GB+. Those aren't for you, and that's fine.

Rent instead of buying

A 16 GB card costs hundreds. An hour of cloud GPU costs cents, and for trying a model that's all you need. If after testing you find you use it daily, then buy — but by then you know exactly which one.

It's also the honest way to write about this: we don't tell people to buy hardware before they know they'll use it.
